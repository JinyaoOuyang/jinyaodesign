## Overview

**Independent design concept · Sole designer · 3 months**

I explored how a culturally rooted tea brand could translate into a North American mobile ordering experience. The work covers menu discovery, drink customization, and pickup guidance.

I owned product framing, information architecture, interaction flows, and Figma designs. This is an independent concept with no engineering implementation or brand affiliation.

## The Design Question

How could a new customer discover unfamiliar drinks while still ordering efficiently? I examined mobile commerce patterns to explore the tension between cultural storytelling, customization depth, and a clear path to purchase.

<Figure src="/work/Problem-framing.png" alt="Design framing for a mobile tea-ordering experience" caption="Exploration framing: connect product discovery, customization, and the pickup journey." />

## Key Design Decisions

### Pace customization step by step

The proposed flow reveals drink options progressively rather than presenting every choice at once.

**Trade-off:** guidance narrows the immediate decision, but adds steps for returning customers who already know their order.

<Figure src="/work/progressive-customization-flow.png" alt="Concept screens for progressive drink customization" caption="Concept flow: introduce customization choices in sequence." />

### Put discovery beside a direct ordering path

The landing experience combines product entry points with space for brand context. The intention is to let interested customers explore while keeping ordering easy to locate.

**Trade-off:** storytelling and promotional content compete for attention. Their placement needs validation through actual ordering tasks.

<Figure src="/work/growth-oriented-landing-experience.png" alt="Concept landing screens balancing product discovery and brand content" caption="Landing concept: product discovery and brand introduction share a clear hierarchy." />

### Continue the flow through pickup

Order status, arrival guidance, and a QR-based pickup concept extend the experience beyond checkout.

**Trade-off:** the interface depends on operational processes. Pickup behavior would need to be designed with staff before implementation.

<Figure src="/work/digital-to-physical-pickup-flow.png" alt="Concept order-status and pickup screens" caption="Pickup concept: communicate what happens after an order is placed." />

## Outcome & Next Steps

The deliverable is a mobile interaction concept. The design hypotheses concern comprehension, ordering effort, and pickup confidence; no conversion lift is claimed.

I would test first-time and repeat ordering separately, then review pickup assumptions with people running the service. That would show where guidance helps and where a faster path is needed.
