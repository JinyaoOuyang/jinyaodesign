## Overview

**Independent PM interview case · 2 weeks · Strategy and prototype**

This project explores how a charging interface could help drivers choose a station under uncertainty. I developed it independently for a Tesla China PM interview, covering the journey, recommendation logic, interface concepts, and a proposed validation plan.

The work is a concept exercise, with no Tesla affiliation or production rollout.

## The Problem

A station list leaves several questions unresolved: will a charger be available on arrival, how long will the stop take, and which option best fits this trip?

I framed the opportunity around reducing the effort of comparing uncertain options while keeping the driver in control.

## Key Design Decisions

### Show a small set of explainable recommendations

The concept ranks two or three options by time, cost, and trip fit. Each recommendation explains its trade-off and can be dismissed.

**Trade-off:** fewer options simplify comparison but depend on reliable station data and a ranking the driver can inspect.

### Explore two levels of detail

A concise recommendation supports a quick decision; a comparison view exposes more detail when needed. Automatic switching between these views remains a hypothesis to validate.

<Figure src="/work/tesla-charging-solution-CarPlay.png" alt="Concept charging recommendations in an in-car interface" caption="Concept screen: charging guidance in an in-car context." />

### Extend the journey beyond station selection

Charging progress, departure reminders, and a summary of the stop connect the recommendation to its outcome. An initial MVP would use explicit rules before introducing predictive models.

## Proposed Validation

These are evaluation measures, not observed results:

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Question</th><th>Measure to establish</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Is choosing easier?</td><td>Decision time and comparison errors</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Are recommendations understandable?</td><td>Comprehension of the stated reasons</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Do drivers trust the choice appropriately?</td><td>Acceptance, overrides, and explanations for both</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Does the stop match expectations?</td><td>Difference between expected and actual wait and charging time</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

Baselines and target values would require real driver sessions and operational data. I would test recommendation clarity and user control before committing to adaptive layouts or broader network optimization.

## Artifacts & Reflection

The deliverables are a strategy deck and interface prototype. The central lesson was to connect every recommendation to an explanation, an override, and a way to evaluate whether it helped.
