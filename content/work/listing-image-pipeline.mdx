## Overview

**Commercial workflow · 6 SKUs · 36 images delivered**

I built a repeatable workflow for FLEXI-CASA's Amazon product imagery. A six-image structure, Figma templates, and reference-guided generation turned individual design tasks into a production system I could reuse across products.

I owned the slot structure, layout templates, generation constraints, visual review, and final content preparation.

<Divider />

## The Problem

Each SKU needed a coherent set of images explaining the product. Creating every image independently meant repeatedly deciding what to show, rebuilding layouts, and checking whether the product still looked accurate.

The opportunity was to **standardize each image's purpose while preserving product-specific evidence**.

<Divider />

## Key Design Decisions

### 1. Give each slot a job before generating content

The system separates the hero image, dimensions, product details, and lifestyle scenes. Templates define the hierarchy; each product supplies its own photography, specifications, and copy.

**Trade-off:** a fixed structure constrains composition. It also gives reviewers a clearer basis for judging whether a slot has the right content.

<Figure src="/work/listing-template.png" alt="Six Figma image templates for a product hero, dimensions, lifestyle scenes, features, and set contents" caption="Template exploration: six image roles establish a repeatable structure before final content is produced." />

### 2. Ground generation in the actual product

I used Gemini's multimodal input with product references to constrain appearance. Text prompts alone left too much room for changes to the product's shape or material.

**Trade-off:** reference input improves control but still requires review. Generated output must be checked against the source product, particularly across variants.

### 3. Keep layout and review in Figma

Figma's component control and export workflow made it the production surface for arranging, comparing, and refining a complete listing set.

**Trade-off:** generation and layout remain separate steps. That extra review stage makes inconsistencies visible before export.

## The Production System

## Outcome & Limits

**Delivered: 36 images across six SKUs, PP-A14 through PP-A19.** The artifacts show a reusable template system, a complete batch, and imagery on an Amazon listing.

<Figure src="/work/Amazon-showcase.png" alt="Amazon product listing showing the FLEXI-CASA paddle set and its image gallery" caption="Listing example: product imagery in the shopping context it was created for." />

The delivery count measures output. Production-time savings and sales impact would need a consistent baseline and measurement before being reported as results.

## Reflection

Two issues remain: visual consistency across SKUs and copy drifting between slots. My next step is to generate a structured product brief first, then feed every slot from that shared source of facts and language.

The system's value depends on how much review it removes without losing product fidelity. That is the next thing to measure.
