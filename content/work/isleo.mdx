## Overview

**Independent project · Working iOS build · 4-minute read**

Isleo is a focus companion where each day becomes a hexagonal island tile and each month becomes a world. Reaching a daily goal grows paradise; missed goals leave cursed terrain, while future days wait under fog.

I designed the experience and built the React Native app, including its map interactions, animated asset system, and session persistence. The current build runs on iOS Simulator and personal devices; beta distribution and App Store release remain ahead.

## The Problem

I designed Isleo around a question: **could a place worth returning to make a focus routine feel more meaningful?**

The starting hypothesis was that a growing world could give accumulated effort a visible identity. This remains a product hypothesis to test with users.

That created two constraints: the world needed enough variety to reward repeated visits, and interrupted sessions needed to preserve progress. As a solo builder, I also needed reusable assets rather than a new illustration for every date.

## Key Design Decisions

### 1. Let the artwork overlap; keep tap targets predictable

**Problem.** Island artwork extends beyond its hexagon. Bounding-box hit areas let a neighboring tile capture a tap intended for another day.

**Decision.** I separated visual bounds from interaction geometry. Figma coordinates define the layout; the app resolves taps to the nearest hex center and rejects taps beyond a distance threshold.

**Trade-off.** Tapping an overhanging tree does not necessarily select the tile that owns that artwork. The hex centers provide a consistent rule while preserving the islands' layered appearance.

### 2. Build the reward into the asset structure

**Problem.** A flattened tile image could fade in, but could not show a pond forming before trees grew.

**Decision.** I authored ground, pond, plants, and shadow layers in Blender under shared material and lighting rules. Reanimated stages their appearance in the app: land, lake, then flora.

**Trade-off.** More layers require stricter export conventions and coordination. In return, the same kit supports date-based variations and an editable reveal sequence.

### 3. Treat returning to a session as part of the experience

**Problem.** A timer's successful completion is only one path. Leaving the app, losing connectivity, or returning after the goal is met also needs a defined outcome.

**Decision.** Local checkpoints preserve counted focus time. Progress below the goal is stored as partial; reaching the goal produces paradise. Local persistence precedes cloud sync, so a slow network does not hold up saving.

**Trade-off.** Recovery and sync add state-management work. I prioritized continuity because the island's reward depends on trusting the progress underneath it.

## From Design to Code

The implementation follows **foundations → UI primitives → product components → screens**. Semantic colors connect the Figma states to the app; `ClayButton` and `IconButton` share reusable behavior, while tile components retain their own art direction.

For example, the day sheet reads session data to select a domain state and its semantic colors. The corresponding layered tile handles the visual reveal. Keeping those responsibilities separate lets the artwork change without rewriting the progress rules.

Month-world generation is an optional experiment. The core map and focus loop use kit assets and remain usable without a generated month-end image.

## Demo & Outcome

**Delivered:** a working iOS build with a monthly map, focus sessions, day details, layered reveals, and local progress persistence.

**Technical validation:** five automated session-save checks pass. They cover delayed cloud responses, failed sync, upload ordering, logout during pending writes, and local storage failure. These checks validate persistence logic under controlled conditions.

## Reflection

The most consequential work was connecting expressive artwork to predictable interaction and durable state. Animation depended on the asset schema; confidence in the reward depended on session recovery.

Next, I want to test whether people understand the map, can reliably select a day, and feel encouraged to return after a missed goal. The cursed state needs particular scrutiny: its emotional effect is still an open question. Beta feedback should guide that decision before I expand the world-generation system.
