The loop

A dramatically shorter engineering feedback loop.

Real-world testing gives you one run per attempt, weeks apart. Antioch runs evaluation as a continuous stage of development, integrated with your existing CI/CD workflows — so iteration speeds up while dependence on expensive real-world testing goes down.

01

Calibrate

Ingest logs, CAD, and sensor specs. Antioch fits a digital twin against recorded runs until simulated and measured behaviour agree within tolerance.

02

Generate

Realistic test scenarios are written from your operational design domain, then perturbed toward the conditions most likely to produce a failure.

03

Execute

Thousands of simulations execute in parallel in the cloud, triggered from CI. Results are deterministic, seeded, and reproducible from a run ID.

04

Analyse & propose

Failures are analysed and clustered by root cause, then returned as proposed improvements across software, firmware, hardware, and AI models.

Output returns to step 01 as new calibration evidence
Why numbered here and nowhere else. Numbering encodes a real dependency — step 03 cannot run before 01 — so the markers carry information. The vertical cards below are unordered and deliberately carry no numbers.
Twin fidelity

A twin you can argue with a regulator about.

Simulation is only worth the compute if the twin is trustworthy. Antioch measures divergence between recorded and simulated runs continuously, and reports it as a number your safety case can cite.

pass 1 → pass 9fleet: 34 vehiclesΔ mean 1.8%
Calibrated, not configured
Twin parameters are fit from your recorded data rather than entered by hand, and re-fit as new field logs arrive.
Reproducible by run ID
Every result carries its seed, twin version, and scenario definition. Any run can be replayed exactly, months later.
Divergence as a metric
Fidelity is reported per subsystem, so you know which parts of the twin your evidence can lean on.
